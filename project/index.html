How does health expenditure relate to life expectancy?

Why this matters

Every year governments and households across the OECD spend billions of dollars on hospitals, doctors, medicines and prevention. A common assumption is that the more a country spends, the healthier its people will be. This website will compare health expenditure per person with life expectancy at birth for 19 selected OECD countries.

Spending is shown in US dollars at purchasing power parity (USD PPP). PPP adjusts for differences in prices between countries, so $1 buys roughly the same amount of health care everywhere. This makes it fair to compare a country like Mexico with a country like Switzerland.

Health Expenditure vs Life Expectancy

Each circle is a country. Hover for details, click to select a country.

Most countries sit in the top middle of the chart: life expectancy rises quickly up to about $5,000 per person and then levels off. The United States stands out far to the right because it spends much more without living longer.
